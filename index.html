The short version: my background is finance and teaching, not a bootcamp certificate, and both taught me to make numbers make sense to someone else.

I come to data analysis through two disciplines: finance and teaching, and both taught me the same lesson. A number only matters once someone can act on it.

Between 2024 and 2025, I worked as a Data Analytics Mentor with ALX South Africa, teaching Python, SQL, and Power BI to aspiring analysts through tutoring sessions, peer workshops, and code reviews. I started in the role as a volunteer and was later brought on paid, which told me the work was landing. My cohort finished with a 79% pass rate, which forced me to get good at explaining data concepts to people encountering them for the first time. That's the habit I bring into every dashboard I build: if a stakeholder can't follow it, it doesn't matter how correct it is.

Before that, as a Data Capturer at Intusol Technologies, I used SQL daily to build and maintain databases and pull the numbers stakeholders actually needed, not just the ones that were easy to find.

My earlier background is in finance: nearly a decade reconciling bank statements and financial records as a Cash Administration Officer at Lancet Clinical Laboratories, which is where my attention to detail comes from. I later completed an ACCA Diploma in Accounting and Business, and I'm now studying toward a BSc in Data Science at IU International University, expected 2028. That combination, finance discipline, teaching clarity, and hands-on SQL and Python work, is what I bring to every analysis: numbers that are accurate, explained simply, and pointed at a decision.

A case study, not a screenshot

One end-to-end example of turning a raw dataset into decisions a business can act on: the problem, the cleaning, the analysis, and what to do about it. More case studies are in progress.

Online Retail Sales Analysis

Read Case Study GitHub ↗
01
Business Problem
A UK-based online giftware retailer had 536,500 raw transaction records but no way to say which customers or products actually drove revenue, or whether the business was seasonal, growing, or data-quality-compromised.
02
Dataset
13 months of transaction line items: invoice, product, quantity, price, customer ID, country. 4,372 customers, 3,940 products, 38 countries.
03
Analysis
Cleaned and documented every transformation in Python and pandas, then ran EDA on revenue trends, seasonality, customer concentration, and returns.
04
Deliverables
Cleaning log, 7 EDA charts, 9 quantified insights, 6 business recommendations, and an 11-slide stakeholder presentation with full speaker notes.
05
Insight
The top 20% of customers generated about 75% of revenue, and November alone drove roughly double the average month's revenue.
Business impact: Quantified exactly where Q4 staffing, a loyalty program for top-tier customers, and checkout data-capture fixes would move revenue. Full detail in the case study.
